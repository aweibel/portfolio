okay so this is the fleet onboarding thing at Routewise, I was there from like early 2022 into 2023, I was the lead product designer on the admin side. Routewise does routing and dispatch software for delivery companies, mid size fleets mostly, like regional grocery and parts distributors. So the problem was setting up a new customer was awful. Like the admin had to go through this setup wizard, I think it was fourteen steps, uploading drivers, vehicles, depots, shift rules, all of it, and people would just bail halfway. Our implementation team was basically doing it for them on calls, which is super expensive, and sales was promising go-live in two weeks and it was taking way longer. So the business wanted to cut implementation cost and get customers live faster.

I worked with a PM, Dana, and the onboarding squad, I think six engineers, and we borrowed a researcher part time. I did the interviews myself mostly, we talked to I want to say twelve admins, some new customers mid-setup and some who'd churned. Also I sat in on a bunch of the implementation calls which was eye opening. The big thing we learned was admins don't have all the data at once, the driver list lives with HR, the vehicles with the fleet manager, so they get stuck at step four or five waiting on someone and then the wizard times out or they lose their place. One admin said something like "I feel like I'm doing someone else's homework." That kind of stuck with me.

So the idea was kill the linear wizard and make it a checklist where you can do things in any order, save and come back, and invite a teammate to do their part, like send the vehicle upload to the fleet manager. We also did a CSV importer that tells you what's wrong row by row instead of just failing.

What didn't work, we first tried a smarter wizard with branching and it tested badly, still felt like a tunnel. And the first importer version tried to auto fix data and people didn't trust it, so we switched to showing the problems and letting them fix.

There was a big fight with sales honestly. The sales VP loved the wizard because it demoed really well, it looked guided and fancy. They didn't want us to remove it. I ended up making a demo mode, and we rolled the checklist out to new customers first so we could compare. Engineering also pushed back on the invite-a-teammate thing because of permissions, it touched the roles model, so we scoped it to a limited "task assignee" role first.

Impact, I don't remember the exact numbers, but setup got way faster and implementation calls dropped a lot, and the CS team was really happy. It became the default for all new customers. I think it also helped with a couple big deals.

If I did it again I'd bring sales in earlier instead of fighting them later, and I'd instrument the old wizard before we changed it because we didn't have a good baseline.
