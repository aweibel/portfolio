Overview

Most designers can tell a great story about their work out loud. Writing it down is where the metrics, the messy middle and the politics disappear.

This skill closes that gap in three moves.

  1. Draft from what you said. Ramble, dictate, paste notes. It sorts everything into ten sections, writes in the first person, and leaves visible placeholders wherever a fact is missing. It never fills the gaps with guesses.
  2. Interview you for the rest. It scores each section, then asks the five questions that would improve it most, starting with impact: baseline, result, timeframe, and where to look the numbers up.
  3. Put it on your page. It finds your own case-study template, whether a site, a CMS or a doc, maps the sections into it, and keeps the page marked "coming soon" until the gaps are filled.

The structure follows what people who hire designers say they look for: the Nielsen Norman Group survey of 204 UX hiring managers, Etsy's and Google's design hiring guides, a former head of product design, a design hiring manager, and a former recruiter. The sources are listed in the reference below.

Use

Just ask. "Turn my notes on the checkout redesign into a case study", "help me write up this project for my portfolio" or "what's my case study missing?" all start it. So do "portfolio piece", "project write-up", "portfolio entry" and "work sample". You can also type /case-study.

Examples

The TL;DR it aims for

Illustrative, from the skill's own guidance. The problem, what I did and the result fit in two sentences.

A tension, told at the right depth

Sales wanted the wizard kept. Our VP of Sales believed the wizard helped close deals, because it demoed well. I didn't argue taste; I brought five session recordings of admins quitting at step 9 and proposed keeping a "demo mode" for sales calls. We shipped the checklist to new accounts first, which let the numbers settle it.

Reviewers say a project with no friction reads as edited. The skill gives disagreements and failed ideas their own sections.

How it marks what it doesn't know

  • METRIC median time from signup to go-live, before → after, first 90 days
  • ASK who pushed back on removing the wizard, and why?
  • IMAGE before/after of the setup screen, with the 3 removed steps circled

These are visible in the draft and hidden on the published page. A recruiter who asks about a made-up number ends the interview.

The kind of question it asks

"You said there was no baseline on the old wizard, but you rolled the checklist out to new customers first so you could compare. What did that comparison show? Ideally for each one: before → after, over what period, and where it came from … If you don't have them, Dana, an old QBR or launch deck, the implementation team's call logs, or Slack might."

Quoted from the live run below. It builds on what you said, asks for every part of a metric, and says where to look.

Docs

The ten sections

SectionWhat goes in it
SummaryA 1–2 sentence TL;DR (problem → what I did → result) and three key takeaways
ContextWho it's for, the business stakes, why now, constraints and scope
RoleWhat I owned, what the team did, partners by function
ResearchMethods, the people behind the personas, a real quote, insight → decision
ProcessDirections explored, why one won, how it was validated
SetbacksWhat didn't work, dropped concepts, pivots
TensionsA real disagreement, both sides fairly, how it was resolved, the trade-off
SolutionThe final design and the craft details that make it yours
ImpactBusiness and user outcomes: baseline → result, timeframe, how it was measured
RetroWhat I learned and what I'd do differently

Rules it follows

  • Nothing invented. Every number, quote and outcome must trace back to what you said. A checker script compares every number in the draft against your words.
  • Hedges get confirmed. "I think it was fourteen steps" is used but goes on a "to confirm" list.
  • Five questions a round, at most. People answer five questions well and fifteen badly.
  • Your raw notes stay private. They're kept in .case-study/, away from anything that gets published. That matters when they name an NDA client.
  • NDA-aware. Clients become "a large US insurer", and shot lists say to recreate screens, not screenshot them.
  • Your layout, not its own. It maps the ten sections into your template and saves the mapping to case-study.config.json, so the next case study goes straight in.
